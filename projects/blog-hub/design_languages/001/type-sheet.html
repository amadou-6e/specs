"A tool for writers demands typography that knows how to step aside."

BlogHub sits between the writer and their audience. The interface must be confident enough to feel professional, and restrained enough to disappear during focused work. The typographic energy is editorial authority held at arm's length — present, structured, never loud.

Every font choice here is a different answer to the same question: how do you make writers trust their workspace without the interface competing with what they wrote?

01 Interface

Functional clarity

System fonts read at zero latency. No character, no friction — the UI becomes invisible and the article status and platform data occupy exactly the attention they deserve. The viewer feels: this is a professional workspace. Best for dense, data-forward screens.

02 Editorial · active

Warmth with discipline

Georgia italic in headlines introduces a human hand — it says "articles are crafted things, not just content." The sans body keeps the workspace efficient. Together they tell the viewer: what's written here is worth publishing, and published with care. The mood is a quality notebook.

03 Technical

Signal over style

All-monospace collapses the distinction between data and prose, treating both as equal information streams. Creates a terminal-like authority. The viewer reads: this tool is for people who want to see what's real. Best for developer-facing or status-first screens.

04 Luxury

Reverential distance

Palatino's generous letterforms slow the reader down intentionally. Combined with sparse layout, the signal is: this content is worth your full attention. Works only when surface area per article is large and image-forward. Collapses under density.

03 — Readability

⟷

Line length

45–75 characters per line. Beyond 75 the eye loses the return.

≈ 560–700px @ 14px
↕

Line height

Headings need less air than body. Mono is dense — always add more.

Heading 1.20 · Body 1.55 · Mono 1.65
Tt

Minimum size

Nothing visible below 9px. Below 11px add UPPERCASE + letter-spacing.

Min 9px · <11px → CAPS + ls +.08
◑

Contrast

WCAG AA: 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large (≥18px or ≥14px bold).

Normal ≥ 4.5:1 · Large ≥ 3:1
W

Weight discipline

Max 3 weights per screen. Pair weight + size — size alone barely registers.

300 · 400 · 500 · (600 accent only)
⋮

Spacing rhythm

All type margins and gaps are multiples of 4px without exception.

04 — Dos & Don'ts

Do

✓Scale onlyNo off-scale sizes. If 13px is too small, raise weight or spacing, not the number.
✓Mono for all dataNumbers, statuses, tags, timestamps — always monospace, every time.
✓Left-align bodyCenter only for single-word labels or empty states.
✓Weight + size together14px/500 over 12px/400 reads as clear hierarchy.
✓Test 1× and 2×Retina-clean can blur or feel heavy at 96 dpi.

Don't

✕Mix 3+ typefacesSans + Mono is the base. Serif is optional. Never all three on one card.
✕weight 700 for body700 is titles only. 500–600 for emphasis.
✕All-caps long stringsTwo words max in CAPS. Article titles in uppercase are illegible.
✕Body below 11pxTags may reach 9px with CAPS. Excerpts and platform names stay ≥11px.
✕Off-grid spacingOne 10px margin breaks the vertical rhythm for everything below it.
